# Deb — Product Requirements
*Product-focused. The intention behind every piece. July 2026.*

---

## What Deb is

Deb is a personal operating system for one person: an AI mentor with a name,
a memory, and a spine, wrapped around the four things a full life actually
requires — knowing what happened, knowing where things stand, deciding and
doing, and understanding what it all means.

The thesis is two of your convictions merged: **impact** (success = turning
goals into reality) and **presence** (stress less, get more done, feel more
alive). Deb exists to close the gap between your goals and your days — and
to make sure you're alive for the days.

She is the seventh attempt, built deliberately on the six before it: TRUE's
honesty and memory, Kairos's calm and presence, SwipeWrite's decisive
triage, familyOS's quiet reliability, the original Deb's personality, and
MyOS's discipline. Everything in this document earned its place through
something that worked — or died — in one of those.

## Deb herself

One mind. She knows your whole life — every world, every goal, every
promise, everything you've ever told her, and what TRUE knew before her
(inherited, labeled as such). Her one law is truth over comfort: she tells
you what's real, with dated receipts, and you should occasionally feel
called out — if you never do, she's failed. She acts without ceremony (say
it, it exists; everything undoable), stays silent when silence serves you,
never initiates, never scores, never guilts. Warm, brief, dryly funny when
the moment earns it. Full law: the Soul Document.

## The shape: one rail, four rooms

**The rail** (left edge, always present) is the one filter. The silver dot
is your whole life; each colored dot is a world — CTDI, Subseven, ISO,
150 Poplar, Family, Personal, and any you create (your color, your call).
Tap a world and you step inside it: the entire app repaints in its color
and every room narrows to it. Tap silver and life widens back out. The
intention: *where you are* is one decision, made in one place, impossible
to mistake — the paint always tells you.

**The rooms** (top, four verbs) are what you're doing. They share one spine
— the same tasks, goals, record, and memory — four faces of one thing.
Nothing exists in two rooms; each room does one verb completely.

### READ — what happened
*The intention: your days deserve a record you'd actually want to reread.*

Today as a page in a book. Everything that flows in — your reMarkable
pages, your Plaud calls, filed notes — lands here as clean, readable
entries: **Deb's distillation on the page, the verbatim original one tap
beneath it** (nothing is ever only a summary; the raw is always kept).
And in the margin, in her own hand, Deb annotates — four kinds of note,
each with a job:

- **The receipt** — today connected to the record, with dates.
- **The read** — her honest take on what an entry really means.
- **The keep** — something she's holding for you (a promise, a free
  afternoon, a chase date).
- **The question** — when evidence is thin, she asks instead of asserts.

Restraint rules: not every entry earns a note — one sharp annotation beats
five. And **every margin note is a door**: tap it and you're in Reflect,
mid-conversation, with that exact thing on the table. Scoped to a world,
Read shows that world's pages — a project journal for free.

You don't type here in V1. The reMarkable is the pen; Read is where the
days land.

### REVIEW — where things stand
*The intention: a warm room where you check on a world without being asked
to do anything about it.*

The Study feeling: fire going, clean shelves, good light. At the silver
dot, six dossiers at a glance — each world with its status line and
momentum. Step into a world and you're reading its dossier: the **mission**
in its own words over the mantle, **Goals** with honest status, **Recently**
— a dated ledger of what actually happened, **Next** — what's coming, drawn
from the same queue React works from, and **Waiting on** — who owes you
what, chase dates attached.

Everything derived, nothing maintained, **nothing tappable into action** —
read-only is the feature. This is where you sit back. The moment Review
lets you edit, it becomes a workspace, workspaces become chores, and chores
die (we have the graveyard to prove it). To act, you walk to a different
room.

### REACT — decide and do
*The intention: every open loop in your life gets a fast verdict, and the
next move is always dealt.*

One stack of cards, one at a time — SwipeWrite reborn as a whole room.
Two species share the stack: **things pulled from your own notes and
calls** ("From this morning's reMarkable page…") and, later, **things from
other people** (emails, Slacks, Teams — each wearing its sender and an ON
YOU verdict). Same gesture either way, because the question in your head
is the same: *what's the verdict on this?*

The grammar is the four D's:

- **→ Do** — it's real and it's mine: onto the Line for today.
- **↑ Delay** — real, not now: it sleeps until its day.
- **← Delegate** — someone else's: hand it off, a chase date starts.
- **↓ Delete** — not a thing: gone, and the extractor (or the mail filter)
  learns from every one.

Beneath every card, the **punch**: done, gone, next dealt. Deciding flows
into doing with no seam — clear the verdicts and the room starts dealing
you the moves, each with Deb's one-line *why*. The payoff for an empty
stack is a plain warm sentence, never confetti: *"Clear. Every loop has a
verdict. Go live it."*

Two protections, both learned the hard way: **the stack is never a second
inbox** — things from people enter only when they clear a high bar (the
ball is on you, it truly matters, or you asked to be told); the other forty
emails stay in Gmail, unpunished. And **there is exactly one queue** — the
Line. Deb speaks the top of it when you ask "what now?", the Reflect room
glances it (two chips above the composer), React deals all of it. Three
zoom levels, one truth, no second list anywhere, ever.

### REFLECT — what it means
*The intention: one conversation, for life, with someone who knows all of
it and tells you the truth.*

The thread with Deb. No bubbles, no chrome — your words right-aligned and
plain, hers in the serif. Saying something actionable just *does* it — "I
owe Larry an invoice" and the task exists, her reply carrying the
confirmation, an Undo pill as the safety net. No confirmation dialogs
anywhere except the two permanent verdicts (done forever, dropped forever),
which get the app's one solemn moment.

This is where the Friday check-in happens (a conversation, not a
ceremony), where "show me my blindspots" summons the invisible record,
where margin notes land when tapped, and where the quote hangs over
everything — context-aware, freezable, occasionally exactly right. Deb can
choose silence here; not every message wants a reply.

## The daily rhythm (all opt-in, zero ceremony)

Morning: open React or ask "what now?" — the Line is right because last
night's page closed the day. During the day: capture without thinking
(write on the reMarkable, record on the Plaud, tell Deb) — it all lands in
Read, distilled, and mints cards for React. When cards pile up: thirty
seconds of the four D's. Friday: ten honest minutes in Reflect. That's the
whole system. No streaks, no scores, no notifications, nothing that
punishes a missed day — the app is incapable of guilt by design.

## What Deb will never have

The graveyard laws, written in the blood of six apps: no scores or streaks
or "won days." No push notifications, no nudges, nothing self-initiated.
No dashboards demanding maintenance. No second inbox. No AI that edits
your structure beyond what you said. No confetti. Silence is a feature;
deletion is always undoable; the raw original is never destroyed.

## V1 and what waits

**V1:** the rail and four rooms · Deb complete (voice, hands, memory,
inheritance from TRUE) · sources: Plaud (auto) and reMarkable (one tap) ·
the stack with note-species cards · the Line · People/Waiting-on ·
the record building invisibly beneath.

**Deliberately waiting:** email/Slack/Teams as card species · calendar ·
Arc (the app lit by the real sun — dawn to midnight) · voice capture's
dedicated moment · proactivity of any kind, if ever.

*The one-line test for every future feature: which room does it belong to,
and does that room stay one verb? If the answer is unclear, the answer
is no.*
